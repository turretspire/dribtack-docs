Privacy Policy

Effective date: May 2, 2026 · Last updated: 26 October, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the Go Gino iOS app (the “App”, formerly called Dribtack) handles information about you. “We”, “us” and the “Publisher” mean the developer who publishes the App, an individual based in Pakistan, whom you can reach at the email address in §18. We’ve tried to keep it readable: where we use a defined term, we mean what it says here.

The short version

Go Gino keeps your data on your iPhone and in your own private iCloud. We don’t run a server, there are no Go Gino accounts, and we don’t collect your name, email or contact details (unless you email us). If you turn on Apple Health, the App saves your finished workouts there and reads back only the workouts it saved. The only third-party service in the App is RevenueCat, which we use for purchase analytics. When you open the App, whether or not you buy anything, it receives a random identifier made by the App, your device’s vendor identifier, your IP address and app and device details, plus the details of any purchase, restore or renewal. It never receives your name, your email, your health data or anything you log.

1. Scope and roles

This Policy covers the Go Gino iOS app, the data processed in connection with it and the emails you send us. It doesn’t cover websites, services or apps run by Apple, RevenueCat, Google, GitHub or anyone else, even if you reach them from inside the App. Those follow their own terms and privacy policies, and we aren’t responsible for them, except for the work RevenueCat does for us (§6).

For the small set of pseudonymous purchase analytics described in §6, and for the emails you send us, the Publisher is the “controller” under EU and UK GDPR. For data that lives only on your device, in your private iCloud or in Apple Health, we are not a controller, because we have no access to it.

2. Data stored on your device

Almost everything Go Gino knows lives on your iPhone and is processed there. We don’t send it to any server of ours (we don’t run one). This includes:

  • Your profile: an optional name; your gender, and your date of birth, height and weight if you add them; your fitness level, goals, body focus areas, injuries and equipment; how you like to train (split, rep range, effort and session length); the days and time of day you train; the activities you like; your units; the weights you usually lift, if you tell Gino; and your bar and plate settings.
  • Your workouts: workouts, exercises and sets (weight, reps, time, distance, rest, warm-up, drop-set and failure marks, how many reps you had left, and when each set was saved), notes, saved workouts, exercises you create and the exercises you mark as favourites.
  • Your body data: weigh-ins with any notes, body fat if you log it, and progress photos with their date, angle and optional weight. Photos are reduced in size before they’re saved.
  • What the App works out from them: personal bests, streaks, badges and milestones, the body map, Gino’s tips and planned numbers, and the text of your reminders.
  • Settings each device keeps for itself: for example whether setup is finished here, your appearance choice, whether Apple Health is on and which workouts were already sent to it, which tips and celebrations you’ve seen, and the App Store’s last answer about prices (so the Plus screen doesn’t jump while it loads).

None of this leaves your device unless iCloud is on (§3), you turn on Apple Health (§4), or you choose to share or export something (§8). Like all app data, it’s also included in your iPhone’s backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to your computer), if you make them.

3. Data stored in your iCloud

If you’re signed into iCloud and iCloud is on for Go Gino, the App keeps the data in §2 (other than the settings each device keeps for itself and your scheduled reminders, which iOS holds on the device) in your private CloudKit database. This means:

  • The data sits in your iCloud account, under your Apple Account, and counts toward your iCloud storage.
  • The Publisher can’t read it. Private CloudKit databases aren’t visible to app developers.
  • Apple processes it as the operator of iCloud, under Apple’s Privacy Policy and the iCloud terms.
  • Sync, conflicts and deletions across your devices are handled by Apple. The App only reads and writes records.

A few small values are also kept in iCloud key-value storage: a copy of whether setup is finished and whether you’ve seen the first Plus screen (each device still goes by its own setting), and the random identifier described in §6, so that all your devices share one.

Deleting the App removes the copy on that device. Your iCloud copy stays until you delete it, and comes back if you install the App again (see §13).

4. Apple Health

Go Gino can work with Apple Health (HealthKit). This is off until you turn it on in You → Apple Health, and iOS asks for your permission first.

  • What the App writes: each workout you finish, as a strength training workout with its start and end time, if you allow it, an estimate of the active energy you burned (worked out on your device from your body weight, or 70 kg if you haven’t added it, the kind of exercise and how long you trained), and an internal reference so the App can recognise its own workouts later.
  • What the App reads: only the workouts it saved itself, so the same workout is never added twice. It doesn’t read any other Health data, such as steps, heart rate, body weight or sleep.
  • What it never does: Health data is not used for advertising or marketing, is not sold, is not shared with RevenueCat or any other third party, and is not stored by the App in iCloud. The Publisher never receives it.

Workouts written to Apple Health are kept by Apple under your Health settings. You can change or withdraw the App’s access at any time in the Health app, and deleting your data in Go Gino doesn’t remove workouts already in Health.

5. Other Apple services

Go Gino relies on several Apple services. Apple is an independent controller of the data it collects through them. We don’t receive identifying information from Apple, but you should know that:

  • App Store: Apple handles your download and any reviews you leave. We see aggregate App Store Connect analytics, the reviews you leave and, if you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, crash reports from Apple that don’t identify you.
  • StoreKit 2: when you buy or restore Plus, Apple processes the purchase. The App receives a signed transaction record (the product, transaction identifiers, dates and whether a free trial or offer applies), which it uses to tell whether Plus is active and when a trial ends. The RevenueCat SDK in the App also passes these transaction records to RevenueCat for the purchase analytics in §6. The App never receives your payment details.
  • Notifications: workout reminders and “Rest is up” are local notifications scheduled by iOS on your device and never leave it. CloudKit may send silent pushes to wake the App for sync. Go Gino doesn’t send marketing notifications.
  • Live Activities: while you rest, iOS shows the countdown on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with the workout’s name and your next set. It’s drawn on your device; no push server is involved.
  • Photo picker: Apple’s picker runs outside the App, so Go Gino gets only the photo you choose and never has access to your photo library.
  • Mail and Safari: if you tap Help → Email us, your Mail app writes the message and we receive only what you send. These documents open in Safari inside the App and are hosted on GitHub Pages (§11).

6. RevenueCat (purchase analytics)

To understand sales, conversion and renewal, the App includes the RevenueCat SDK. RevenueCat acts as our processor for purchase analytics: the App connects to it when you open it, whether or not you buy anything, and it sees the App Store purchases made in the App and gives us dashboards and events about them. Your purchases themselves are checked by Apple’s StoreKit, not by RevenueCat.

What RevenueCat receives

CategoryWhat exactlyWhy
Pseudonymous user ID A random identifier the App makes on its first launch. It’s saved on your device and in your iCloud key-value storage, so your devices on the same Apple Account share it. What RevenueCat does not receive

  • Your name, email or any other contact details (the App never asks for them or sends them).
  • Your Apple Account, card details or other payment information, which stay with Apple.
  • Your workouts, body data, photos, Health data, profile or anything else you create in the App.

Under EU and UK law, an IP address and a device identifier still count as personal data even though they don’t name you, so we describe them plainly here rather than calling the integration “fully anonymous”. RevenueCat’s own practices are described at revenuecat.com/privacy. RevenueCat processes this data only on our behalf, under its data processing agreement with us. We confirm that RevenueCat provides the same or equal protection of your data as this Policy describes and Apple’s App Store Review Guidelines require. Our legal basis, and your right to object, are in §9 and §14.

7. Data we do not collect

To be clear, the Publisher does not:

  • Run any server, database, login or account system. There is no Go Gino account.
  • Collect your name, email, phone number, postal address, voice or biometric data. (If you email us, we receive your email address and message; see §8.)
  • Receive your workouts, profile, body data, progress photos, Health data or anything else you create in the App.
  • Use advertising, attribution or product-analytics SDKs (such as Mixpanel, Amplitude, Firebase Analytics, AppsFlyer, Adjust, Branch or the Meta SDK). RevenueCat is the only third-party SDK in the App, used only as described in §6.
  • Track you across other companies’ apps and websites. The App doesn’t ask for App Tracking Transparency permission.
  • Sell, rent or trade any data.

8. How information is used

Because most of your data never leaves your devices or your iCloud, “use” in the privacy sense covers only a little:

  • Inside the App, on your device: to build your saved workouts, plan your weights and reps, draw the body map, work out personal bests, badges and progress, write Gino’s tips, schedule reminders, and everything else you use the App for.
  • Share cards: if you share a workout, a new best, a streak, a weekly goal, your week or a before-and-after card, the image is drawn on your device and handed to iOS’s share sheet. A before-and-after card can include the progress photos you pick for it. Once you choose where to send it, that app handles the image under its own terms. We never receive a copy.
  • Export: if you export your workouts, the spreadsheet files are made on your device and go wherever you send them.
  • Emails you send us: we use your address and message only to reply and to help you.
  • RevenueCat analytics: to run, understand and improve Plus, set prices, decide on free trials and follow cancellations and refunds.

9. Legal bases (EU and UK GDPR)

If you’re in the EU, EEA, UK or Switzerland, our legal bases for the limited personal data we do process are:

  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR): the RevenueCat analytics in §6 (understanding sales as a whole and improving the App), and answering your emails. We balance this against your privacy by keeping the data to a minimum, not linking it to your name, and having RevenueCat process it only under a data processing agreement. You can object to this at any time, or ask for the data to be deleted, by email (§14).

If the law ever requires us to keep or disclose data (§11), that legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR) is the basis. Everything that stays on your device, in your iCloud or in Apple Health needs no legal basis on our side, because the Publisher doesn’t process it.

10. Permissions

The App may ask for these iOS permissions. You can change each of them at any time in the iOS Settings app:

  • Notifications: workout and rest-day reminders, and “Rest is up” when a rest ends. Scheduled and shown on your device only.
  • Apple Health: only if you turn it on, to save your finished workouts (see §4).
  • Photos (add only): only if you tap Save Image on a share card, to add that image to your photo library. This permission lets the App add photos, not see them, so the App still can’t read your library.
  • Live Activities: the rest countdown on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
  • iCloud: set at the system level; you can turn it off for Go Gino in iOS Settings.

Choosing a progress photo needs no permission, because Apple’s photo picker gives the App only the photo you pick.

11. Sharing

We share personal data only as this Policy describes:

  • With Apple, as the operator of the App Store, StoreKit, iCloud, CloudKit, Apple Health and the iOS notification system.
  • With RevenueCat, as our processor for the purchase analytics in §6. RevenueCat uses its own sub-processors (such as cloud hosting providers), listed in its data processing agreement.
  • With Google, which hosts our email (Gmail), so emails you send us are stored there under Google’s Privacy Policy.
  • With law enforcement or regulators, where the law requires it, or where we need to establish, exercise or defend legal claims, and only where we actually hold the data asked for, which for your activity in the App is very unlikely.
  • With a successor, if all or part of the Publisher’s business is merged, sold or restructured. A successor would continue to use the data as this Policy describes unless it tells you of a change (§17).

Our support pages, including this Policy, are hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any website host, GitHub logs the IP address of anyone who opens them, for security, under GitHub’s privacy statement. We don’t use cookies, analytics or trackers on these pages.

12. International transfers

RevenueCat is based in the United States. Where personal data of EU, UK or Swiss users goes to RevenueCat, we rely on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (with the UK Addendum or Swiss equivalent where relevant), together with RevenueCat’s technical and organisational measures. The Publisher is based in Pakistan and reads the analytics in §6 and the emails you send from there. Apple, Google and GitHub run global infrastructure and decide where the data they process is held under their own policies.

13. Retention and deletion

Data on your device is kept until you delete it or delete the App. Data in your iCloud is kept until you delete it: deleting the App doesn’t remove it, and it comes back if you install the App again. To delete everything, use You → Delete all data while you’re signed into iCloud with iCloud on for Go Gino. This removes your workouts, saved workouts, weigh-ins, progress photos, personal bests, the exercises you created and your profile from your device and your iCloud, and the deletion carries through to your other devices signed into the same Apple Account. It doesn’t remove workouts already saved to Apple Health (delete those in the Health app) or your purchases, which stay with your Apple Account. Copies in your iPhone’s backups stay until those backups are replaced or deleted.

RevenueCat keeps the records it holds for us (the App’s random identifier, your device’s vendor identifier, and purchase, app and device details) for as long as our RevenueCat account is open, under RevenueCat’s retention policy, unless you ask us to have them deleted. RevenueCat uses your IP address to work out your country and doesn’t keep it on your record. To ask for RevenueCat’s records about your purchases to be deleted, write to us (§18) with the Order ID from Apple’s receipt email (also listed at reportaproblem.apple.com). We’ll use it to look for the record and ask RevenueCat to delete it. If you never bought anything, RevenueCat’s record holds the App’s random identifier, your device’s vendor identifier, app and device details and the country worked out from your IP address. None of it names you, so we have no way to match it to you. If you keep using the App afterwards, RevenueCat starts a new record the next time you open it.

Emails you send us are kept in our email account for as long as we need them to help you and to keep a record of the conversation.

14. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or get a copy of your personal data, to object to or restrict its processing, to withdraw consent, and to complain to your local data-protection authority. Because most of your data lives on your own device, in your own iCloud and in your own Health app, you can use most of these rights yourself, by editing, exporting or deleting data in the App, or through your iCloud and Health settings.

For the limited data in §6, write to us (§18) and we’ll reply within the time the law requires (usually one month under GDPR).

You can object at any time to the RevenueCat analytics in §6, or ask for that data to be deleted, by writing to us (§18). We’ll ask RevenueCat to delete the record we can find for you (§13). The App can’t switch RevenueCat off for one person, so to stop it sending anything further, delete the App.

If you’re in Brazil, you have the rights in Art. 18 of the LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados), including confirmation that we process your data, access, correction, anonymisation, blocking or deletion, portability, information about who we share it with, and the right to object and to complain to the ANPD. Our legal basis for the RevenueCat analytics is legitimate interest (Art. 7, IX). Write to us (§18) to use these rights.

If you live in California, you have rights under the CCPA and CPRA, including the right to know, delete and correct, and to opt out of the “sale” or “sharing” of personal information. We don’t sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising.

15. Children

Go Gino isn’t for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 13. If you’re 13 or older but a minor where you live, use the App only with the consent and supervision of a parent or guardian, as our Terms require. If you think a child under 13 has sent us personal data, write to us (§18) and we’ll delete what we can find.

16. Security

We rely on Apple’s platform security (iOS sandboxing and data protection, and iCloud encryption in transit and at rest) for data on your device, in iCloud and in Apple Health, and on RevenueCat’s security programme for the analytics in §6. No system is perfectly secure, and we can’t guarantee that data won’t be lost or accessed without permission, so we encourage you to keep your iPhone, iOS and Apple Account protected with current software and a strong passcode, Face ID or Touch ID.

17. Changes to this Policy

We may update this Policy from time to time, and we’ll change the “Last updated” date above each time. When we make an important change, we’ll also mention it in the App or in the App Store release notes before it takes effect, unless the change is needed sooner for legal or security reasons.
